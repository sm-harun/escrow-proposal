01

Proposal overview

Executive Summary

EthioAdera respectfully submits this proposal to Commercial Bank of Ethiopia for consideration of a strategic banking partnership to support a digital escrow model in Ethiopia. The central request is for the Bank to assess and, if legally and operationally permissible, establish five separate dedicated escrow account arrangements, each aligned to one of EthioAdera’s proposed service categories.

The five requested account categories are: (1) Real Estate Escrow, (2) Construction Escrow, (3) Online Escrow, (4) International Trade Escrow, and (5) Mergers and Acquisitions Escrow. EthioAdera proposes separate accounts rather than one general pooled operating account, so that the Bank and the parties can distinguish funds by transaction category, subject to the legal structure approved by the Bank.

Under the proposed model, the Bank would provide the regulated deposit-taking and banking infrastructure. EthioAdera would provide a digital transaction workflow through which parties can establish transaction terms, receive status information, submit supporting documents and coordinate authorized payment or refund requests. The Bank would retain its own approval, verification, compliance and execution responsibilities as required by law and the agreed mandate. EthioAdera would not independently withdraw, transfer, invest, lend, or use customer funds.

The proposal is deliberately conditional. Before launch, the parties must agree who is legally entitled to open and hold each account, how funds are legally characterized, whose instructions the Bank may act upon, what constitutes a valid release instruction, how disputes are handled, and whether any license, authorization, regulatory no-objection or other approval is required.

EthioAdera seeks an initial meeting with the Bank’s business, legal, compliance, risk, operations and information-technology teams. The purpose of that meeting is to test feasibility, identify the correct account structure, define conditions precedent, and determine whether a controlled pilot can be designed.

Decision requested

  • Appoint a bank relationship lead and a cross-functional review team.
  • Assess the feasibility of five separately designated escrow account arrangements.
  • Advise on the lawful account-holder, mandate, safeguarding and regulatory structure.
  • Discuss deposit, release, refund, dispute, reconciliation and reporting procedures.
  • Explore secure technical integration and a limited pilot after all approvals and controls are in place.

05

Why a structured arrangement

Market Context and Rationale

Transactions involving high-value assets, staged performance, remote counterparties or complex delivery conditions often require more than a simple transfer from one party to another. The parties may need a neutral process to document the conditions under which funds are to be held and paid. Examples include payment after title transfer, staged construction milestones, confirmation of goods delivery, inspection of imported machinery, or completion of a corporate transaction.

A bank partnership can combine the Bank’s deposit-taking, payment execution, customer due diligence and risk capabilities with a technology platform that organizes transaction information and makes the agreed process easier for customers to follow. The arrangement should be designed so that the Bank does not rely on unsupported platform claims and EthioAdera does not perform regulated banking activities without the required authority.

Strategic relevance

Ethiopia’s digital payments policy direction emphasizes a more integrated, trusted and inclusive digital financial ecosystem. The National Bank of Ethiopia’s published National Digital Payments Strategy materials describe priorities around interoperability, trust, resilience, consumer protection and innovation. These policy themes provide context for exploring responsible digital transaction services, but they do not themselves grant EthioAdera permission to operate an escrow service or guarantee that the proposed account structure will be approved. [1]

The Bank’s participation could allow it to assess a new source of transaction activity and customer engagement while retaining control over the banking functions it performs. EthioAdera’s platform could help organize the commercial instructions and transaction evidence associated with those flows, subject to a clear division of legal responsibilities.

Why five categories

The five service lines involve materially different transaction patterns, evidence requirements, time horizons, risk profiles and settlement triggers. Separate dedicated account arrangements are proposed to make these categories easier to distinguish and govern. The Bank may recommend a different account architecture if its legal, accounting, operational or regulatory analysis requires it.

06

Business rationale

The Transaction Problem and Proposed Response

Challenges the proposed service seeks to address

  • Counterparty uncertainty: one party may be reluctant to pay before the other party performs.
  • Performance timing: payment may depend on delivery, inspection, milestone certification or transfer of ownership.
  • Evidence fragmentation: agreements, receipts, inspection reports and approvals may be spread across email, paper and messaging channels.
  • Dispute exposure: parties may disagree about whether a condition has been met or whether a refund is due.
  • Limited transaction visibility: parties may not have a single status record showing deposit confirmation, pending conditions and settlement history.
  • Complex transactions: multi-party projects and corporate deals may require staged payments and carefully controlled releases.

Proposed response

EthioAdera proposes to provide a structured workflow that records the transaction terms, identifies the parties, assigns a unique transaction reference, records the agreed conditions, tracks evidence and presents the transaction status. The Bank would receive deposits into the appropriate approved account and execute payments only under the account mandate and procedures agreed with the Bank.

The digital platform is intended to reduce avoidable confusion and improve recordkeeping. It cannot eliminate commercial risk, guarantee the performance of a buyer or seller, independently determine disputed facts, or replace legal advice. The parties remain responsible for their underlying contract and for providing accurate information and evidence.

Expected customer experience

  1. The parties agree the commercial contract and escrow conditions.
  2. The transaction is created and assigned a unique reference.
  3. Required parties complete identity and due diligence checks.
  4. The payer deposits funds using a Bank-approved channel and reference.
  5. The Bank confirms receipt; the platform reflects the verified status.
  6. The parties complete the agreed performance steps and submit evidence.
  7. Authorized release or refund instructions are reviewed under the mandate.
  8. The Bank executes an approved payment and provides confirmation.
  9. The transaction record is closed and retained for audit and dispute handling.

Five service categories

Proposed Escrow Service Portfolio

1

Real Estate Escrow

Proposed use: purchase or sale of residential, commercial or land-related property where payment is linked to documented conditions such as due diligence, title verification, contract milestones or transfer documentation. Evidence may include signed sale agreements, identity and authority documents, property records, legal clearances and written approvals. The Bank should determine which documents it can reasonably verify and which remain the responsibility of the parties and their legal advisers.

2

Construction Escrow

Proposed use: construction contracts with staged payments linked to measurable milestones. Potential evidence includes signed contracts, approved work schedules, engineer or consultant certificates, inspection reports, invoices, variation approvals and completion certificates. The Bank should not be expected to certify construction quality; technical certification should come from the contractually appointed qualified professional.

3

Online Escrow

Proposed use: online purchases of goods or services where payment is held pending agreed delivery, inspection or acceptance conditions. The workflow may record product descriptions, seller details, shipping or delivery evidence, acceptance windows and refund rules. The service must clearly define what happens when the buyer does not respond, delivery is delayed or the goods are materially different from the agreed description.

4

International Trade Escrow

Proposed use: selected trade transactions involving importers, exporters, suppliers or buyers, subject to Ethiopian foreign-exchange, trade, customs, sanctions and cross-border payment requirements. This category is especially dependent on the Bank’s trade-finance, foreign-currency and compliance capabilities. No foreign-currency account or cross-border settlement is assumed to be available under this proposal.

5

Mergers and Acquisitions Escrow

Proposed use: corporate transactions in which a portion of the purchase price is retained pending completion conditions, indemnity periods, regulatory approvals, closing deliverables or other negotiated obligations. Such transactions require specialist legal documentation, clear beneficial ownership, corporate authority checks, tax and regulatory review, and carefully defined release events.

The five categories are proposed service lines, not a statement that all five can be launched simultaneously. The Bank may recommend a phased approach, with lower-complexity domestic use cases considered first and higher-risk or cross-border categories deferred until the relevant legal and operational requirements are satisfied.

Funds-flow principle

Customer funds should move from the payer to the Bank through a Bank-approved payment channel and into the legally approved escrow arrangement. Funds should not pass through EthioAdera’s ordinary operating account. EthioAdera should not have unilateral authority to redirect, withdraw or use those funds. A payment to the beneficiary should be made by the Bank only after the agreed release requirements and the Bank’s own checks have been satisfied.

Any technical status displayed by the platform must distinguish between “payment initiated”, “payment received”, “funds cleared”, “release requested”, “release approved”, “payment executed”, “refund pending” and “transaction closed”. A platform notification must not be presented as a Bank confirmation unless it is based on an authenticated Bank response.

Protection and control

Customer Funds Safeguarding Principles

Customer funds are central to the proposed arrangement. The Bank and EthioAdera should agree a safeguarding design that is legally valid, operationally workable and transparent to customers. The proposal seeks separation between customer transaction money and EthioAdera’s corporate operating funds, but the precise legal method and its effect must be confirmed by counsel and the Bank.

Minimum design principles for discussion

  • Purpose limitation: funds received for an escrow transaction are used only for the purpose and outcomes defined in the governing documents.
  • Segregation: the approved account structure and accounting records distinguish customer funds from EthioAdera’s own funds and from unrelated customer flows.
  • Restricted access: EthioAdera staff do not have ordinary withdrawal rights or unilateral transfer authority.
  • Dual control: material release and refund instructions are subject to authentication and approval controls appropriate to the risk.
  • Transaction traceability: each deposit and payment is linked to a unique transaction reference and supporting record.
  • Reconciliation: balances are reconciled regularly between Bank records and EthioAdera’s transaction ledger.
  • Exception handling: unmatched funds, reversals, fraud alerts, court orders and disputes are escalated and handled under documented procedures.
  • Customer disclosure: terms explain who holds funds, what protection is and is not provided, release conditions, fees, dispute channels and risks.

Legal questions requiring explicit answers

The definitive agreements should state who owns or beneficially controls funds at each stage; whether the account is held in trust, agency, custodial, client-money or another legally recognized form; whether funds are protected from claims against EthioAdera or any intermediary; how insolvency of any party affects balances; whether the Bank may exercise set-off or liens; how interest is allocated; and how court orders, attachments or regulatory directions are treated.

EthioAdera does not ask the Bank to promise protection beyond what applicable law and the final account structure actually provide. Customer-facing descriptions must match the legal effect of the signed arrangement.

Compliance framework

AML/CFT, KYC and Customer Due Diligence

The proposed service may involve individuals, companies, beneficial owners, agents, brokers, developers, contractors, importers, exporters and other counterparties. The Bank must determine the customer relationship and the due diligence obligations that apply to each account and transaction. EthioAdera must not assume that collecting a form or verifying an email address satisfies the Bank’s KYC requirements.

Proposed compliance workflow

  1. Identify each relevant party and the capacity in which that party acts.
  2. Collect the information and documents required by the Bank and applicable law.
  3. Identify and verify beneficial owners, authorized representatives and signatories where required.
  4. Conduct screening against applicable sanctions, politically exposed person and other risk lists as required.
  5. Assess transaction purpose, source of funds and expected activity on a risk-sensitive basis.
  6. Apply enhanced due diligence where the Bank or applicable law requires it.
  7. Monitor for unusual patterns, third-party funding, structuring, rapid movement or inconsistent transaction behaviour.
  8. Escalate suspicious or prohibited activity to the responsible compliance function and follow legal reporting and confidentiality rules.

Division of responsibilities

The Bank determines its own customer acceptance standards, screening rules, transaction monitoring and regulatory reporting obligations. EthioAdera can support the process by collecting information through approved channels, maintaining accurate transaction metadata, flagging workflow anomalies and promptly responding to Bank requests. The final agreement must specify which party collects each data element, who verifies it, how updates are handled and how long records are retained.

The parties should agree a prohibited-activity policy, transaction limits, high-risk sector rules, geographic restrictions, escalation timelines and procedures for freezing, rejecting or returning funds where legally permitted. The International Trade and M&A categories should receive enhanced specialist review before being offered.

14

Customer protection and exceptions

Disputes, Refunds and Exceptional Events

Escrow does not remove the possibility of disagreement. It creates a controlled process for dealing with funds while the parties perform their contract. The account mandate and customer agreement must clearly distinguish an ordinary release request from a disputed transaction and specify who has authority to decide what happens next.

Illustrative dispute process

  1. A party raises a dispute through the designated channel and identifies the transaction reference and grounds.
  2. EthioAdera acknowledges receipt, preserves the transaction record and notifies the other party in accordance with the agreed terms.
  3. The platform marks the transaction as disputed and prevents routine automated progression.
  4. The Bank is notified where the dispute affects a pending payment or requires a Bank-side restriction.
  5. The funds are held, restricted or otherwise treated only to the extent permitted by the account mandate, applicable law and any court or regulatory order.
  6. The parties may resolve the matter by joint written instruction, an agreed independent determination, arbitration, court order or other mechanism expressly set out in the contract.
  7. The Bank executes the resulting instruction only after verifying its validity and completing required checks.

Refund and reversal scenarios

The parties must define refunds for failed delivery, cancelled transactions, duplicate deposits, overpayments, payment reversals, seller non-performance, expired transaction windows and mutual cancellation. A refund should ordinarily return funds to the verified source account or another destination permitted by the Bank and applicable law. Any exception requires documented rationale and enhanced verification.

Exceptional events

Procedures are also required for death or incapacity of a party, insolvency, court attachment, regulatory instruction, sanctions alert, suspected fraud, lost credentials, platform outage, Bank system outage, payment network reversal, unmatched funds and a party becoming unreachable. The Bank’s legal obligations and valid legal orders prevail over platform workflow settings.

Systems and connectivity

Technology Integration and Operating Model

EthioAdera proposes a limited and secure integration with the Bank, subject to the Bank’s technical standards, architecture, security review and approval. The objective is to reduce manual reconciliation and improve the accuracy of transaction status information without weakening Bank control or exposing sensitive systems.

Potential integration capabilities

  • Unique transaction reference: a reference that links the Bank deposit record to the platform transaction.
  • Deposit confirmation: authenticated confirmation that funds have been received and, where relevant, cleared.
  • Transaction status: agreed status messages for pending, received, held, released, refunded, rejected or reversed transactions.
  • Statement and reconciliation data: secure delivery of transaction and balance information at an agreed frequency.
  • Instruction submission: controlled submission of release or refund requests with supporting metadata, subject to Bank authentication and approval.
  • Exception notification: alerts for failed deposits, unmatched references, reversals, compliance holds and processing errors.
  • Audit trail: time-stamped records of requests, responses, approvals, rejections and status changes.

Integration options to assess

The Bank may prefer a secure API, host-to-host file exchange, bank portal, maker-checker portal workflow, or a manual controlled process during an initial pilot. EthioAdera is open to the Bank’s preferred architecture. No assumption is made that the Bank currently offers a particular API or that real-time integration will be available.

Operating model

The parties should define operating hours, cut-off times, business-day calendars, settlement expectations, service windows, escalation contacts, incident notification deadlines, maintenance windows, support ownership and change-management procedures. The design should include a safe manual fallback for critical operations, with independent verification and subsequent reconciliation.

Technology safeguards

Information Security, Privacy and Resilience

Because the proposed platform may process identity information, contractual documents, payment references and transaction evidence, information security and privacy must be built into the service from the beginning. The Bank should conduct its own technology and third-party risk assessment and may require independent testing before integration or launch.

Baseline security controls for discussion

  • Role-based access and least privilege for employees, administrators and support personnel.
  • Multi-factor authentication for privileged access and sensitive customer actions.
  • Encryption in transit and at rest using Bank-acceptable standards.
  • Secure software development, code review, vulnerability management and change control.
  • Centralized security logging, monitoring, alerting and protected audit records.
  • Segregation of development, test and production environments; no live customer data in test unless approved and protected.
  • Periodic penetration testing and remediation tracking by competent independent testers.
  • Secrets management, key rotation, certificate management and secure API authentication.
  • Backup, recovery testing, business continuity and disaster recovery procedures.
  • Staff confidentiality commitments, background checks where appropriate and regular security training.

Privacy and data governance

The parties should document the purpose and lawful basis for each category of personal data, the party responsible for each processing activity, access and correction procedures, retention periods, secure deletion, cross-border hosting restrictions if applicable, breach notification and cooperation with lawful requests. Customer consent should not be used as a substitute for identifying the correct legal basis or statutory duties.

Resilience

The service should have defined recovery objectives, backup frequency, recovery testing, outage communications, transaction freeze procedures and a reconciliation process for transactions interrupted during an outage. The Bank must remain able to operate its own account controls independently of EthioAdera’s platform.

Financial control

Reconciliation, Reporting and Auditability

Accurate reconciliation is essential to demonstrate that platform records correspond to actual Bank-held funds and executed payments. EthioAdera proposes transaction-level reconciliation using Bank statements or authenticated reporting, supported by unique references and exception management.

Reconciliation framework

  1. Obtain Bank-provided statement or transaction data through an approved channel.
  2. Match each Bank entry to a unique escrow transaction reference and platform record.
  3. Identify unmatched, duplicate, reversed, partial or incorrectly allocated entries.
  4. Place exceptions into a controlled queue with an assigned owner and target resolution time.
  5. Investigate discrepancies using source documents and Bank confirmation—not assumptions.
  6. Record the resolution, reviewer and supporting evidence.
  7. Produce daily or periodic reconciliation certification as agreed with the Bank.

Proposed reporting pack

  • Opening and closing balances for each approved account arrangement.
  • Deposits received, payments released, refunds, reversals and outstanding balances.
  • Transaction ageing and pending release conditions.
  • Disputed and restricted transactions, with status and ageing.
  • Unmatched deposits and reconciliation exceptions.
  • Failed or rejected instructions and reasons where disclosable.
  • Customer complaints, fraud alerts, security incidents and operational errors.
  • High-value or unusual activity escalated to the Bank under agreed procedures.

The Bank retains its own books, records, statements, audit rights and regulatory reporting. EthioAdera’s platform ledger is a supporting operational record and must not be represented as the Bank’s official account ledger. The parties should agree record retention periods, access rights, audit cooperation, evidence preservation and procedures for regulatory or external audit requests.
